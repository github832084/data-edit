# Expressive 2D Cutout-Puppet Animation

## Visual direction

**Reference match:** IMG_1044.PNG and IMG_1045.PNG; secondary reference IMG_1040.PNG.

**Look:** Bold illustrated characters with rounded faces, thick outlines, visibly segmented limbs, simple expressive eyes and warm palace interiors. Build an original articulated-paper-puppet look, with a polished cinematic environment behind it.

**Palette and rendering:** Burnt orange #B96924, deep brown #29110B, ochre #D99B3D, skin tones chosen per character, burgundy #6F201C, and emerald #247552 accents. Use consistent 5–8 px outlines at final resolution. Use subtle background gradients but mostly flat character fills.

**Characters:** Draw original front and three-quarter model sheets with separated upper arms, forearms, hands, head, torso and legs. Position pivots inside shoulders, elbows, wrists and hips. Use visible circular joint details only as an intentional design choice. Create neutral, concern, determination and surprise expressions; keep facial features and costume coordinates stable.

**Animation grammar:** Rig anticipation, action and settle: a raised hand needs a shoulder rotation, elbow bend, wrist follow-through and a torso shift. Use 4–6 mouth shapes only for actual spoken character dialogue; do not make every person lip-sync the narrator. Add blinks, gaze changes, breathing and purposeful reactions. Constrain joint angles to avoid broken elbows. Use pose-to-pose acting with brief holds rather than constant bobbing.

**Framing:** Use medium shots for gesture-led storytelling, close-ups for decisions, and occasional full-body staging for movement. Keep faces in the upper-middle area and hands above the subtitle band. Build palace depth using separate wall, arch, floor and lamp layers. Match eyelines across conversational cuts.

**Caption treatment:** Use bold Noto Sans Devanagari or a clean sans serif, white with a dark stroke, one yellow emphasis word, at y=1320–1500 when clear. Do not imitate a platform username, logo or creator signature. Keep the same subtitle anchor through a conversation.

**Music and SFX:** Use muted tabla or frame-drum pulses, a low string/plucked drone and sparse reed or flute phrases at 80–100 BPM. Tie cloth rustles, wood/dice taps or footsteps to visible contact. Use comic accents only when the narration is deliberately playful; never add laughter to a serious event.

**Preferred implementation:** This is a strong code-rendering option. Create original layered SVG characters with explicit pivot coordinates and render a deterministic animation timeline using the available engine. Use joint animation plus replacement face drawings. Test a five-second gesture shot before constructing the full sequence.

**Shot example:** Illustrative 5-second shot: a courtier glances at an object in their palm, closes the fingers, turns toward another character, then lifts an eyebrow. Three-quarter medium composition, one restrained camera push, two synchronized object-contact sounds. Keep the narrator off screen unless explicitly requested.

**Style rejection checks:** Reject visible gaps at limb joints, hands detached from props, random lip flapping, changing moustache shapes, and rigid torsos with moving forearms only.

## 4. Generate and animate

Create a 4–6-second representative shot internally, inspect it, and repair the approach before producing the remaining scenes. Continue without waiting for a new approval unless the user or tool requires one. Reuse successful assets and rerender failed shots only.

Make each action-bearing beat visibly animated: changing pose, a turn, a gesture, steps, interaction or a coherent action cycle. Camera zooms, sparkles and moving captions alone do not satisfy this. Limited animation is intentional, but it must tell the story through character motion. Allow held poses on quiet moments. Preserve physical contact, ground placement, screen direction and facial identity. Render titles and subtitles in the editor, never inside generative scene art.

## 5. Captions, music and sound

Build UTF-8 SRT captions and an editable styled format such as ASS or the project's native text timeline. Use phrase groups of roughly 3–7 words, no more than two lines, normally 0.8–2.5 seconds depending on speech. Avoid rapidly flashing single words. Split Hindi at word boundaries, never through a conjunct or matra. Check complex-script shaping and use an available licensed font with proper glyph coverage.

At 1080×1920 start with 54–68 px caption type, adjusting for actual font and language. Use a working content-safe rectangle around x=90–900 and y=240–1500; this is a conservative editing guide, not a permanent platform guarantee. Check the intended platform's preview when available. Follow the style-specific caption position. Move text away from faces/hands and avoid collisions with headings. Highlight one meaningful word per phrase at most; render captions against the actual audio, aiming for no more than about 100 ms visible drift.

Use original or appropriately licensed instrumental music and SFX. Keep a record of source, license or generation provenance. Do not reuse film soundtracks, unlicensed devotional recordings or fabricated Sanskrit chants. Never claim music is royalty-free without evidence. Design a score arc with setup, tension and resolution where the narration supports it. Place sound effects against visible actions, not random edit points.

Keep narration dominant; begin music roughly 18–24 dB below the voice and adjust by ear with ducking. Avoid harsh de-essing, excessive denoising and repeated loud impacts. Aim for a finished mix around −16 to −14 LUFS integrated and true peak at or below −1 dBTP, treating these as production targets rather than platform requirements. Check both headphones and small-speaker intelligibility. Preserve natural pauses and intelligible final syllables.
