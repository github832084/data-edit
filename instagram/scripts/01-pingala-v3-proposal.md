# Pingala script · research notes and v3 proposal · Nov 27, 2026

## A. What the research says about the facts in v2

| Claim in v2 | What sources say | Change |
|---|---|---|
| The counting rules are 8.28–8.31 (द्विरर्धे । रूपे शून्यम् । द्विः शून्ये । तावदर्धे तद्गुणितम्) | Same numbering in Shah's paper on the Chandaḥśāstra (arXiv math/0703658) | keep; owner still checks his edition |
| "शून्य" here is a marker, not the digit 0 | Plofker (*Mathematics in India*, 2009, pp. 54–56) describes it as a marker, often cited as an early explicit use of *śūnya* | keep, it is our strongest honest line |
| Read the digits "in reverse", like binary | Van Nooten (1993, *J. Indian Philosophy* 21) noted the higher place values are written to the right | keep, and say it plainly: "Pingala wrote the big place on the right" |
| Meru-prastāra = Pascal's triangle, in Halayudha's 10th-century commentary, ~700 years before Pascal | The triangle is clear only in Halayudha's reading of the cryptic sutra 8.34. Weber (19th c.) doubted it goes back to Pingala; Shah argues it can be traced to him. Halayudha is usually dated to the 10th century, though one popular page says the 13th | keep it out of the main video; give it its own short Reel with this nuance |
| "2,000+ years old" | Usual dating c. 3rd–2nd century BCE | keep, owner confirms |

**Verdict:** the facts in v2 hold up. The problems are in the storytelling, not the facts.

## B. Why v2 does not hold attention

1. **No story, only a lesson.** The references that do best tell a story: #1 (Panini, "the slowest pupil"), #7 (Ashwatthama). Ours is a lecture with no person, no problem and nothing at stake.
2. **The payoff comes too late.** Binary only arrives after about 4 minutes. A viewer who came for "your phone" waits too long.
3. **Too many terms.** Prastāra, uddiṣṭa, the counting sutras, Meru-prastāra and Leibniz all fit in 5½ minutes. That is three videos' worth.
4. **Maths by ear is hard.** "6 halved is 3, minus one is 2…" cannot be followed by listening. It must be shown, and kept short.
5. **No moment for the viewer to act.** Nothing asks the viewer to try, comment or send.
6. **The ending does not echo.** We start with a phone and end with a generic "follow". We should end on the phone again.
7. **Missing the Prof's own voice.** Nothing in it is personal: a real classroom moment or why this verse matters to him.

## C. Proposed v3: about 3½ minutes, one story, one reveal

| # | Time | Beat | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0:00–0:15 | **Hook + promise** | phone 0/1 → "this way of counting was written for poetry, 2,000 years ago" + an **open loop**: "by the end, you will turn a Sanskrit line into binary yourself" |
| 1 | 0:15–0:50 | **The problem (stakes)** | the Vedas were oral; *chandas* (metre) is one of the six Vedāṅgas, so a wrong metre was a wrong recitation. Pingala's question: how many metres can exist, and how do you find metre no. 37 without writing a list? |
| 2 | 0:50–1:25 | **The insight** | every syllable is only light (laghu ।) or heavy (guru ऽ): two signs. One real line scanned on screen |
| 3 | 1:25–2:05 | **The list** | 3 syllables → 8 patterns, in Pingala's order; the columns change every 1, 2, 4 rows |
| 4 | 2:05–2:45 | **The reveal (the viewer does it)** | guru = 0, laghu = 1, the big place on the right → the row number. "Pause and try this one." The moment for comments |
| 5 | 2:45–3:15 | **The bonus** | 8.28–8.31 in one shot on screen: halve / mark *śūnya*; computers still use halve-and-square. Śūnya here is a marker, not the digit 0 |
| 6 | 3:15–3:35 | **Honest claim + echo** | "Pingala did not build a computer. He wrote a method that works like binary." Back to the phone. CTA: "write your name's syllables as । and ऽ in the comments" + follow; teaser for the next video |

Moved out to separate short Reels: Meru-prastāra (with the Weber/Shah nuance), and Leibniz 1703 (one line can stay in beat 6).
