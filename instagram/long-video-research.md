# Instagram and long video · research · Nov 17, 2026

## What Instagram has right now

| Format | Length | Shape | Who can post | Reaches non-followers? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reel | up to 20 min | vertical 9:16 | everyone | sources disagree; several say only Reels up to about 3 min are recommended to non-followers |
| **Features** (new, Oct 26, 2026) | up to 90 min | **horizontal 16:9** | test only: selected creators in the US and Brazil | through "Clips as Reels" (below) |

- **Features** was announced by Adam Mosseri (head of Instagram) and Tessa Lyons on Oct 26, 2026. Mosseri said he had been against long video "for a long time".
- **Clips as Reels:** a creator links up to 8 short Reels to one long Feature. The Reel is the trailer, and the long video is where it leads.
- **TV app:** Instagram has a TV app (Fire TV, Google TV, Samsung; Apple TV reported). Horizontal long video is aimed at it.
- **India:** no launch date, no eligibility rules and no way to apply have been announced (Business Today, Oct 27, 2026).
- **Money:** no ad-revenue share. Meta pays a few hand-picked creators directly (reported $50K–$500K). "Too early to share a long-term monetization model."

## The principles Instagram uses to pull in long-video makers

1. **The short Reel finds the viewer; the long video keeps them.** Mosseri's worry was that long video means fewer Reels sent to friends. His answer: Reels travel through DMs, so a Reel that is *sent* becomes the recommendation for the long video.
2. **Sends matter most for reaching new people.** Mosseri named watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach as the top signals. Sends weigh a little more for non-followers, likes a little more for followers.
3. **Only original work is recommended.** Since 2024 for Reels, and since Apr 30, 2026 for photos and carousels: if most of an account's posts in 30 days are someone else's content, the account stops being recommended. A near-copy repost now surfaces the original.
4. **Horizontal and TV-ready.** Features are 16:9, made for the TV app, like YouTube.
5. **Pay selected creators directly, not by views.** Instagram is funding the first long-video makers by hand. There is no open programme to join yet.
6. **AI must be labelled.** Realistic AI video or a realistic AI voice must carry the "AI info" label, set while posting. If it is skipped and Meta detects AI, the label is added anyway. Accounts built around an AI person need an "AI-generated profile" label, or their reach is cut. AI-written scripts and captions need no label.

## What this means for @prof_harikrushn45

- **Today in India:** post the long video as a **long vertical Reel (8–15 min)**, and cut **3–5 short Reels (30–90 s)** from it. The short ones bring new people, and the long one builds trust. Each short Reel ends with: "पूरी कहानी प्रोफ़ाइल पर।"
- **Make it so Features can be added later:** draw every scene so it can also be exported as **16:9**. When Features opens in India, the same video can go up horizontally, and on YouTube as well.
- **Optimise for sends:** end each Reel with a line people want to forward. Fact-check content does this naturally ("send this to whoever forwarded the NASA message").
- **Own drawings, own voice and own research = original.** Never re-upload other channels' clips.
- **Voice:** the Prof's own recorded voice needs no label. If an AI clone of his voice ("avatar") is used, turn on the AI info label. That is honest, and it fits the brand of "the source behind every claim".
