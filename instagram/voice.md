## My positions

1. Many IKS findings are real and impressive, but only when you can name the text and the verse. "The Vedas say" is not a source.
2. Fake claims hurt real ones. Pingala's binary is real; Vedic airplanes (Vaimanika Shastra, written 1918–1923) are not. Mixing them makes both look fake.
3. Keep the claim modest: "works like binary", not "Pingala invented computers".
4. "Vedic Maths" usually means a 1965 book, not an ancient text. Say Indian mathematics when you mean the old sources.
5. Credit should be shared, not stolen: the theorem was "never just Pythagoras", but that doesn't mean Pythagoras copied anyone.
